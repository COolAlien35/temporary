# QuantumLoop
## Five-minute judge demo

**Format:** screen recording with voiceover  
**Target runtime:** about 5:00 after speed adjustment  
**Narration:** record naturally, then speed the final cut to 1.25–1.5×  
**Editing rule:** speed through navigation and setup; return to normal speed for results, collaboration settings, and instructor feedback.

> **Runtime note:** The timestamps below are final-video timestamps. A 1.25–1.5× cut gives more room for narration while keeping the finished demo near five minutes. Leave enough screen time for judges to read the Prediction vs. Actual chart and the collaboration controls.

## 0:00–0:20 · Start with the learner

**On screen:** Open on the landing page. Hold on the animated hero and sign-in card, then scroll to the learning-loop preview.

**Voiceover:**

> Quantum computing can feel abstract when students encounter it only through equations and finished examples. QuantumLoop gives them a place to experiment. A student predicts, builds, runs a circuit, compares the result with their expectation, and decides what to try next. Let’s follow that journey from the learner’s first screen into the labs, circuits, hardware, and classroom.

---

## 0:20–0:45 · A clear next step

**On screen:** Enter the demo. Show the learner dashboard, its recommended next step, and recent progress. Open the curriculum map and pause briefly on its units and progress indicators.

**Voiceover:**

> The learner dashboard makes the next action easy to find: continue a lesson, revisit a topic, or retry a challenge. The curriculum map shows how lessons, practice, labs, and quizzes fit into the course. Students can see where they are and move directly into the next part of their learning.

---

## 0:45–1:50 · Predict, build, run, understand

**On screen:** Open the **Superposition** lab. Briefly show the stages. In Predict, set an outcome distribution and confidence, then lock the prediction. In Build, place or adjust a gate and run the circuit. In Observe, hold on **Prediction vs. Actual** and its numeric differences. Open Explain and ask the Lab Partner: *“Why did the Hadamard change the measured outcomes?”* Show the response.

**Voiceover:**

> Let’s start with superposition. The lab asks the student to predict before seeing a result. I’ll choose the outcomes I expect and record my confidence. Now that prediction is locked, it becomes a hypothesis I can test.
>
> Next, I build the circuit and run it. The observation view compares my prediction with the measured distribution, both in the chart and in the numbers. If the result surprises me, I have something concrete to investigate. I can ask the Lab Partner about the gate and this run, then use the explanation to decide whether to change the circuit and test again.
>
> The loop turns a quantum concept into an experiment: make a claim, gather evidence, explain what happened, and revise your understanding.

---

## 1:50–2:45 · Build circuits together

**On screen:** Open **Circuit Studio**. Show the gate palette and a prepared circuit. Inspect a gate, switch to Split or Code view, run the circuit, and show the output. Click **Collaborate** and hold on the party dialog. Point out party size, who can join, access level, and link expiry. Generate a party link if the demo setup is ready.

**Voiceover:**

> For more open-ended exploration, Circuit Studio gives learners a workbench for building and testing circuits. I can choose gates, inspect operations, and view the same circuit visually, as code, or in a split view. After running it, I can look at the output and inspect the circuit step by step.
>
> The Collaborate control opens the party setup. The host chooses the party size, whether anyone with the link can request a seat or only invited people can join, what access they have, and when the link expires. That makes it straightforward to invite others into a shared circuit session.

---

## 2:45–3:45 · From components to a quantum system

**On screen:** Open **Hardware Catalog**. Filter the list, show a component’s specifications and 3D preview, then the stage compatibility matrix. Open **Hardware Builder**, load **Two-Qubit Pair**, show the diagram, select or route a line, and run a validation. Switch to 3D. Briefly open the builder’s **Collaborate** dialog, showing that the same party controls are available there.

**Voiceover:**

> QuantumLoop also connects circuits to the physical systems that run them. In the Hardware Catalog, students can browse components, inspect specifications and compatible connections, and preview parts in 3D. The stage matrix helps explain where each component belongs.
>
> In Hardware Builder, I’ll load a two-qubit setup. The design places components across the refrigerator’s temperature stages. I can select a signal type, connect ports, and switch between the wiring diagram and a 3D view of the cryostat. Validation and thermal diagnostics help students identify issues in their design.
>
> Hardware designs can also be shared through the collaboration party controls, so a team can work on the system together.

---

## 3:45–4:10 · Progress that reflects learning

**On screen:** Open **Quantum Passport**. Show progress, badges, activity, and prediction calibration. If it is ready, briefly open a challenge result with its feedback.

**Voiceover:**

> The Quantum Passport brings the learner’s progress into one view: activity, badges, assessment history, and prediction calibration. Challenges give students another chance to apply an idea in a new circuit and see feedback on their attempt. They can see what they’ve completed and what they might tackle next.

---

## 4:10–4:50 · The instructor’s workflow

**On screen:** Open the instructor dashboard. Go to assignment authoring, select a circuit, show its instructions and a question, and preview the learner view. Open a prepared submitted assignment and show the learner response and instructor feedback form. Return to the dashboard.

**Voiceover:**

> Instructors can connect these experiments to classroom teaching. From the dashboard, they can review learner activity and assessment progress. In assignment authoring, a teacher can choose a circuit, write instructions, add questions, and preview the learner experience before publishing.
>
> After a student submits their work, the instructor can review the answers and provide written feedback. That creates a clear path from hands-on experimentation to structured practice and personal guidance.

---

## 4:50–5:00 · Close on the full journey

**On screen:** Quick montage: prediction comparison, Circuit Studio collaboration dialog, hardware builder in 3D, assignment review. End on the landing page or logo.

**Voiceover:**

> QuantumLoop helps students predict, build, run, collaborate, and learn from the result—with instructors guiding the journey.

## Recording checklist

- Prepare a demo learner account with enough activity to make the dashboard and Passport meaningful.
- Open a ready-to-run Superposition lab and decide the prediction before recording.
- Prepare a Circuit Studio circuit and a **Two-Qubit Pair** hardware design.
- Prepare one submitted assignment so instructor review is immediate.
- Use 1.25–1.5× for transitions and repetitive setup. Keep charts, dialog settings, diagnostics, and feedback readable.
- If showing a collaborator actively editing, include a second participant joining and making a visible change. The party dialog by itself demonstrates invitation and access controls.
